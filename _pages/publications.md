## Working Papers

### Output without Workers: Automation and Structural Transformation

2026
{: .entry-meta}

Sectoral value-added and employment shares often diverge during structural transformation. In a task-based model with non-homothetic CES demand, price and income effects drive value-added shares, while employment also responds to a third force: a labor-saving displacement effect that reassigns tasks from workers to capital without moving value added at first order. Building on the growth accounting of Jones and Tonetti (2026), we recover the displacement effect from standard national-accounts data alone; an assumption on task-level labor productivity is required only to interpret the recovered term as automation's displacement. In the US, displacement accounts for 64% of the model-predicted agriculture–manufacturing and 35% of the services–manufacturing employment reallocation over 1951–1989, rising to 188% and 73% over 1990–2019. After 1990, labor-saving task reallocation—with automation as its leading source—shifts from agriculture to manufacturing.
